Lil Bro - History Wipe privacy policy

Last updated: 19 September 2026

Lil Bro - History Wipe deletes chosen entries from your browsing history. Everything happens on your device. There is no server behind it.

What it accesses

  • Your browsing history, to find the entries that match your rules and delete those.
  • Extension storage for your rules, your settings, an optional local log of what was cleaned, and the count shown on the settings page.
  • A PIN you set yourself, if you switch the list lock on. It is stored on your device as a hash and never sent anywhere, and it does one thing: it hides your rule list from other people using the same computer. It does not encrypt anything, and anyone who can reach your browser settings can still remove the extension. Forgetting it has a way out: type the word lilbro and the PIN goes, and so does everything the extension saved, your list included. None of that comes back.
  • Cookies, if you use the cookie features. It reads cookie names and domains to delete the ones you did not keep, and keeps the site of an open tab in session storage (memory, gone when the browser closes) so a closed tab's cookies can go with it. Tab access is an optional permission, asked for only when you switch that feature on.
  • Other stored browsing data: cache, download history and saved form text, cleared through the browsing-data permission and only with those switches on.

What it does not do

  • Nothing is collected, sold or shared. No analytics, no account, no third party.
  • It makes no network requests of its own.
  • Everything it runs ships inside the package. There is no remote code, no eval, and nothing fetched at runtime.
  • It does not read page content, and it does not touch passwords: Chrome removed password deletion from extensions, so there is no such switch.

Cookies, cache and the rest

Clearing is off by default. With it on, cache, cookies, site storage, download history and saved form text go for the span you pick, when you press a button, and at startup if you ask for that. Cookies are removed for the whole site, so being signed out of it is the normal result. The keep list is the exception: those sites are never touched. Chrome cannot narrow history, download history or form text to one site, so those are all-or-nothing for the span you choose.

Where your data lives

Your settings, the PIN and the optional log of what was cleaned stay on your machine, in your browser's local extension storage. They are never uploaded anywhere. The log can be cleared at any time from the extension's settings page, and removing the extension deletes all of it.

Your rule list is stored in the browser's synced extension storage. That is what lets a rule you add on one computer apply on your other computers. If you have browser sync switched on, your browser sends that list to the browser vendor's sync service so it can reach the devices you are signed in to. That transfer is made by the browser itself, under your browser account settings: the extension transmits nothing and never talks to a server. With sync switched off, or while you are signed out, the list stays a local file like everything else.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the date at the top of the page changes with it. Continued use of the extension after an update means you accept the current version.
